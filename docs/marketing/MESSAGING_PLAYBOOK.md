# Klovered — messaging playbook

The reusable source of truth for how we talk about Klovered. Anyone writing a
landing page, email, ad, deck, or sales note pulls from here so the language
stays consistent. If a line isn't here and you write a good one, add it back.

Voice rules are inherited from [BRAND.md](../../BRAND.md) §3. This doc is the
*what we say*; BRAND.md is the *how it sounds*.

---

## 0. How to use this

1. Start from the **core promise** (§2) — never contradict it.
2. Pick the **segment angle** (§6) for who you're talking to.
3. Pull **paste-ready blocks** (§8) and swap in the segment's villain + proof.
4. Check it against the **word list** (§9) and **claims discipline** (§10).

The golden rule, one line: **sell what changes for them, not what the product does.**

---

## 1. The core insight

People don't buy features. They don't even buy "what we do." They buy **their
world after us** — and the escape from the world before us.

The world before Klovered: a firm turns down bids it could win because there
isn't time, and burns its most expensive people retyping answers they already
wrote. The world after: they enter every bid worth winning, and their experts
approve instead of author.

Every piece of copy moves the reader from the first world to the second.

---

## 2. The core promise (never changes)

> **Enter every bid worth winning.**

Supporting one-liner:

> Klovered reads the RFP, drafts answers grounded in your own documents, and
> routes them through review. You enter the bid. You don't grind through it.

This is fixed. Segments, campaigns, and channels change the *angle*, never the
promise.

---

## 3. The message spine (every asset has these three)

1. **The villain** — the specific pain moment (segment-specific, §6).
2. **The turn** — Klovered removes it.
3. **The proof** — a number, not an adjective (§7).

If a headline has no villain and no number, it's decoration. Cut it.

---

## 4. Features → outcomes (the translation table)

The single most common mistake is describing the machine. Translate every
feature into the thing it *saves or wins*.

| Don't say (feature) | Say (outcome) |
|---|---|
| AI requirement extraction | Nothing on page 140 gets missed |
| Grounded, cited drafting | Answers you can put your name on |
| No-source flagging | It never makes something up |
| Answer library / reuse | Every bid gets faster than the last |
| Review workflow | Your experts approve, they don't author |
| Template / Word export | Submission-ready, in their format |
| Confidence scoring | You see what's safe to send and what needs a human |

Rule of thumb: if the subject of the sentence is the software, rewrite it so the
subject is the customer or their outcome.

## 6. The segment ladder

One promise, four audiences, four villains. Climb the ladder — land at the top
of the list, expand down it as ISO and enterprise-readiness arrive. Each entry
has a **gate**: don't ship the copy until the gate is true.

### 6.1 IT consultancies & system integrators — *land now*
- **Who:** 5–50 person firms answering 20–50 commercial tenders a year.
- **Buyer = user:** the owner, partner, or bid lead. Buys on a card.
- **Villain:** senior consultants writing bid answers at billable rates.
- **Angle:** "Your best consultant, retyping — instead of billing."
- **Headline:** "Stop paying senior people to retype last year's answers."
- **Proof:** 300 questions in 3 days, not 3 weeks.
- **Gate:** none. Sell today.

### 6.2 Engineering & professional services — *broaden now*
- **Who:** firms where every new client starts with a formal proposal.
- **Buyer = user:** principal / practice lead.
- **Villain:** the constant proposal tax on every new engagement.
- **Angle:** "Every new client starts with the same document. Answer it in a day."
- **Headline:** "Win the client, not the paperwork."
- **Proof:** answers reused and improving every bid.
- **Gate:** none. Same motion as 6.1 — sell in parallel.

### 6.3 Government contractors — *expand when ISO lands*
- **Who:** firms bidding public-sector tenders, 10–40 a year.
- **Buyer:** bid director / sales director. **Users:** bid managers + reviewers.
- **Villain:** one missed requirement disqualifies the entire bid.
- **Angle:** "In a government tender, one missed line loses everything."
- **Headline:** "Miss nothing. Every requirement, tracked and cited."
- **Proof:** 255 requirements found, 100% cited or flagged, 0 invented.
- **Gate:** ⚠️ **ISO 27001 in hand** before using "certified"; DPA + UK/EU data residency ready.

### 6.4 Large enterprises — *upmarket, 12mo+*
- **Who:** big vendors/consultancies with dedicated bid teams.
- **Buyer:** procurement + security + a VP sponsor. Multi-stakeholder.
- **Villain:** bid-team knowledge scattered, ungoverned, walking out the door.
- **Angle:** "Your bid team's knowledge, governed and secure at scale."
- **Headline:** "Enterprise bidding, without the enterprise risk."
- **Proof:** SSO, retention controls, audit trail, data in your region.
- **Gate:** 🚫 **Do not ship.** Requires SOC 2 + SSO/SCIM + retention + residency, none built yet. Copy exists here only so it's ready when the product is.

## 7. Proof bank (reuse these — numbers, not adjectives)

- 300 questions in 3 days, not 3 weeks.
- 255 requirements extracted from a 65-page tender in under 3 minutes.
- 100% of answers cited or flagged. 0 invented.
- Every answer traces to the source passage it came from.
- The bids you skipped last quarter — enter them next quarter.

Rule: a claim without a number is a weaker version of the same claim. Find the
number. Never inflate it (see §10).

## 9. Words

**Banned** (from BRAND.md — never use): seamless, unlock, empower, leverage,
revolutionize, effortless, supercharge, magic, world-class, cutting-edge.
Also avoid: "powerful," "robust," "solution," "streamline," "game-changer."

**Approved / on-brand:** grounded, cited, flagged, reviewed, enter the bid, win,
miss nothing, never guesses, your own documents, in days, tracked, per seat.

**Style (from BRAND.md §3):** sentence case everywhere. Short sentences. Lead
with the outcome. Numbers carry the proof. Contractions fine. No exclamation
marks. No em dashes.

## 10. Claims discipline (legal + trust — non-negotiable)

- **Never claim a certification we don't hold.** No "ISO 27001 certified" or
  "SOC 2" until the certificate is real. "Working toward ISO 27001" is fine;
  "certified" when we're not is fraud and, in a bid, disqualifying.
- The sample knowledge base contains *fictional* certification claims. Never
  copy them into real marketing.
- **Numbers must be real and current.** The proof-bank figures come from real
  runs. Don't round up, don't invent a testimonial we don't have.
- **Don't over-promise the AI.** We say "cites or flags," never "always
  correct." The honesty is the differentiator — protect it.

## 11. Objection → response

| They say | We say |
|---|---|
| "How do I know it won't make things up?" | It doesn't. Every answer cites the source passage, or it flags the gap for a human. No source, no answer. |
| "Is my data safe / where does it live?" | Encrypted in transit and at rest, isolated per customer, hosted in your region. Your documents are never used to train any model. |
| "Do you have ISO / SOC 2?" | *(Pre-cert)* We follow the same controls — tenant isolation, encryption, access control — and we're working toward ISO 27001. We'll sign a DPA today. *(Never claim the cert itself.)* |
| "We already have a process." | Keep it. Klovered does the first draft from your own documents so your experts review in minutes instead of writing for days. |
| "It's another subscription." | One won bid pays for years of it. The real cost is the bids you're skipping now. |
