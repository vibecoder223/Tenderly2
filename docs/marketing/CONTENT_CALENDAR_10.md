# Klovered — LinkedIn content calendar (10 campaigns)

Built on [LINKEDIN_CAMPAIGN_GUIDELINES.md](LINKEDIN_CAMPAIGN_GUIDELINES.md).
Cadence: 3–4 per week ≈ a 3-week runway. Order is deliberate: open with the
demo story, alternate pillars, close with the founder arc. Every visual uses
the pitch-deck system (Clash Display + Geist, one green hue, browser-framed
product vignettes). Production: copy `klovered-linkedin-monday/build-carousel.mjs`,
edit the slides array, run it.

Legend for vignettes (all real UI patterns from the deck):
- **STATS BAND** — 255 questions found · <3 min read-to-draft · 100% cited or flagged · 0 invented
- **CITED CHIP** — `cited · 1.0` (green tint)
- **GAP CHIP** — `no source · needs a human` (amber tint)
- **LIBRARY ROWS** — answers with `reused · 7 bids` chips
- **DRAFT BOX** — cited answer quote + source line

## 01 · The 3-minute reveal — *flagship demo story* (pillar: proof)

**Hook:** I gave the AI a 200-page government tender. Here's what happened in the next 3 minutes.

**Post:**
> I gave the AI a 200-page government tender.
>
> 65 pages of requirements. The kind three people usually lose three weeks to.
>
> Three minutes later: every requirement found. Every answer drafted from our own past proposals. Every claim cited to its source.
>
> The ones it couldn't back up? Flagged for a human. It doesn't guess. In a bid, a guess is a liability.
>
> That's the difference between skipping a tender and entering it.
>
> Send me a real RFP and I'll run it live on yours.

**Carousel (7):**
1. Hook (ink): "200 pages. 3 minutes. Watch."
2. "It read all 65 pages. Found all 255 requirements." (STATS BAND)
3. "It drafted every answer. From our documents, not the internet." (DRAFT BOX)
4. "Every claim cited to its source." (CITED CHIP)
5. "What it couldn't back up, it flagged." (GAP CHIP)
6. "3 minutes vs 3 weeks. That's a bid you enter instead of skip."
7. CTA (green): "Send me a real RFP. I'll run it live."

**Creative direction:** the carousel *is* the demo. Dark hook, then each slide
is one browser-framed vignette with a single-line headline. The swipe should
feel like watching the product work. No decoration; the UI is the visual.
**Images:** browser frames, stats band, chips. **CTA:** DM/send an RFP.

## 04 · The confident lie — *AI trust wedge* (pillar: industry myth / AI education)

**Hook:** A made-up answer in a bid isn't a typo. It's a binding commitment.

**Post:**
> Generic AI will happily write your RFP response.
>
> It will be confident. Fluent. Fast.
>
> And somewhere in those 200 answers, it will state an uptime you don't offer, a certification you don't hold, a feature you never built.
>
> In a bid, that's not a hallucination. It's a commitment your firm now has to honour, or a disqualification when the buyer checks.
>
> The bar for AI in proposals isn't "sounds right." It's "show me where that came from."
>
> Every Klovered answer cites the document it came from, or flags a human. No source, no answer.
>
> Would you rather your AI sound certain, or be checkable?

**Carousel (6):**
1. Hook (ink): "AI wrote your bid. It sounds great. One answer is a lie."
2. "Fluent. Confident. And it promised an SLA you don't have."
3. "In a proposal, a guess becomes a contract."
4. "The bar isn't 'sounds right'. It's 'show me the source'."
5. "Cited, or flagged for a human. Never invented." (CITED CHIP vs GAP CHIP, side by side)
6. CTA (green): "Certain or checkable. Which would you sign?"

**Creative direction:** the tensest campaign — keep it stark. Slide 5 is the
signature visual: the two chips side by side, green vs amber. That one image
is the entire product philosophy. **CTA:** comment (debate bait, deliberately).

## 06 · Copied, not written — *best practices* (pillar: proposal craft)

**Hook:** The average proposal isn't written. It's copied.

**Post:**

> The problem is *how* teams copy: hunting old documents, pasting stale numbers, forgetting the version legal approved.
>
> Copying isn't the problem. Copying badly is.
>

>
> Your next questionnaire is mostly pre-answered. If you can find the answers.

## 09 · Behind the product: why it refuses to answer — *feature deep dive as principle* (pillar: behind the product)

**Hook:** We taught our AI to say "I don't know." Hardest feature we built.

**Post:**
> The hardest feature we built isn't extraction or drafting.
>
> It's the refusal.
>
> When Klovered can't find a source in your documents, it doesn't improvise. It returns "no source" and routes the question to a human.
>
> Commercially awkward, honestly. Demos look shinier when the AI answers everything.
>
> But our users sign what the AI writes. Their names go on the bid. An invented answer isn't a rough edge, it's their reputation.
>
> So: cited, or flagged. Nothing in between. That constraint is the product.

**Carousel (6):**
1. Hook (ink): "We taught our AI to say 'I don't know'."
2. "No source in your documents? It refuses to draft."
3. "'no source · needs a human'" (GAP CHIP, huge, centered)
4. "Awkward in demos. Essential in bids. Your name goes on the answer."
5. "Cited, or flagged. Nothing in between." (CITED CHIP + GAP CHIP)
6. CTA (green): "Should AI ever guess in a legal document? Tell me why."

**Creative direction:** the amber GAP CHIP as hero image on slide 3 — a
product screenshot elevated to a manifesto. Builds enormous trust.
**CTA:** comment. **Hashtags:** #buildinpublic #ai #productdesign #proposals
