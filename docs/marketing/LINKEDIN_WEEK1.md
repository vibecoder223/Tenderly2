# LinkedIn — week 1 plan + scripts

Audience: owners / bid leads at small consultancies, agencies, and professional
services firms (segment 6.1–6.2 in [MESSAGING_PLAYBOOK.md](MESSAGING_PLAYBOOK.md)).
Goal for week 1: **engagement and trust, not sales.** We build an audience of the
right people first. Voice follows BRAND.md — sentence case, short, numbers, no
hype words, no exclamation marks.

---

## The engagement strategy (why this works)

- **Carousels get the most reach on LinkedIn** — people swipe, dwell time is high,
  the algorithm rewards it. We use 3 carousels + 1 hot take + 1 poll this week.
- **Slide 1 is 90% of the job.** If the first slide doesn't stop the scroll, the
  rest is never seen. Every hook below is a pattern-interrupt or a stakes line.
- **One idea per post.** Never two.
- **End every post with a question.** Comments drive reach far more than likes.
- **Reply to every comment in the first 2 hours.** That window sets the reach.
- **Post 08:00–09:30 UK, Tue–Thu strongest.** Consistency beats perfect timing.

Format specs for carousels: **1080×1080 square, 8 slides, PDF upload.** Big text,
one thought per slide, lots of whitespace, brand green accent, mono for numbers.

## MONDAY — Carousel: "The bids you didn't enter last quarter"

**Slide 1 (hook):**
> Your biggest competitor isn't another firm.
> It's the bid you didn't have time to enter.

**Slide 2:**
> Every quarter, your team turns down tenders you actually qualify for.
> Not because you'd lose. Because there wasn't time to answer.

**Slide 3:**
> A 200-page RFP. Three weeks to respond. Your senior people already booked solid.
> So the bid quietly becomes a "no."

**Slide 4:**
> Nobody logs that "no." It never shows up in a report.
> It's revenue you decided to lose, silently.

**Slide 5:**
> Here's the uncomfortable math:
> one won bid can pay for a whole year of answering them faster.

**Slide 6:**
> The fix isn't working weekends.
> It's answering in days instead of weeks, without missing a requirement.

**Slide 7:**
> That's what we're building. Klovered drafts RFP answers from your own past
> proposals. Cited to the source. Never invented.

**Slide 8 (CTA):**
> How many bids did your team skip last quarter?

**Caption:**
> Most firms don't lose bids. They skip them. The tender lands, there's no time to

## TUESDAY — Text (hot take)

> A made-up answer in a bid isn't a typo. It's a binding commitment your firm
> didn't mean to make.
>
> This is why I'm wary of teams pasting RFP questions into generic AI. It writes a
> confident answer whether or not the fact behind it is true. In a proposal, that
> confident guess becomes a contractual promise, a compliance claim, a number you
> now have to stand behind.
>
> The bar for AI in bidding isn't "sounds good." It's "can you show me where this
> came from." An answer should cite the document it's based on, or admit it doesn't
> have one. No source, no answer.
>
> Would you rather your AI sound certain, or be checkable? I'll take checkable
> every time.
>
> #ai #proposals #bidmanagement

## WEDNESDAY — Carousel: "How to answer a 200-question RFP in 3 days"

**Slide 1 (hook):**
> How a 12-person firm answers a 200-question RFP in 3 days.
> Not 3 weeks. Here's the process.

**Slide 2:**
> Step 1 — stop reading page by page.
> Extract every requirement first, so nothing on page 140 gets missed.

**Slide 3:**
> Step 2 — don't start from a blank page.
> You've answered 80% of this before. Draft from your own past proposals.

**Slide 4:**
> Step 3 — cite every claim.
> In a bid, an unsourced answer is a liability. Tie each one to a document.

**Slide 5:**
> Step 4 — flag what you can't back up.
> If the source isn't there, mark it for a human. Never let AI invent it.

**Slide 6:**
> Step 5 — experts review, they don't author.
> Your specialists approve drafts in minutes instead of writing for days.

**Slide 7:**
> The result: days, not weeks. Nothing missed. Nothing invented.
> Your best people stay on billable work.

**Slide 8 (CTA):**
> Which step does your team lose the most time on today?
> Curious how many recognise step 2 as the real bottleneck.

**Caption:**
> The three-week RFP response isn't a law of nature. Most of that time goes to
> reading, hunting through old proposals, and retyping. Here's the process we've
> seen cut it to days, without missing a requirement or inventing an answer. Which
> step is your bottleneck?
>
> #rfp #proposals #bidmanagement #consulting
